CONCEPTLENS MEDICAL DEMO
Guided walkthrough script: four core moments

Audience: research / ML / medical-imaging audience
Target duration: 8-10 minutes
Branch: dev

Before starting
---------------
1. Run ./start.sh and open http://localhost:5173.
2. Keep the medical domain selected.
3. Open Domain Settings and confirm Medical is enabled.
4. Expand Model Trust so the evaluation numbers are visible.
5. In the concept strategy selector, use CBM (Trained Probes).
6. Click Load sample image once. Keep the three sample thumbnails visible.

Opening (30 seconds)
--------------------
Say:
"This is ConceptLens, a post-hoc interpretable wrapper for a chest X-ray
classifier. The goal is not only to show the predicted class, but to expose
an interpretable concept layer, spatial evidence, and controlled
counterfactuals. I will show four things: prediction, concept evidence,
spatial attribution, and a what-if intervention."

Important framing:
"This is a research demo, not a clinical diagnostic tool. The concept
supervision is weak/model-derived rather than radiologist-annotated. The
metrics shown in Model Trust are held-out evaluation results, so they tell us
how reliable the model and concept probes are, not whether this is clinically
validated."

1. Compare three canonical cases (2 minutes)
---------------------------------------------
Action:
1. Click the COVID-19 thumbnail.
2. Click Predict.
3. Point to the prediction, confidence, and concept bars.
4. Click the Pneumonia thumbnail and Predict.
5. Click the Normal thumbnail and Predict.

Say for COVID-19:
"The model predicts COVID-19 with its calibrated confidence. The concept
fingerprint is more informative than the class name alone: bilateral
involvement, lung opacity, consolidation, and ground-glass opacity are the
main evidence signals in this case."

Say for Pneumonia:
"The important comparison is not just that this is abnormal. The concept
profile changes. Concepts such as effusion, edema, cardiomegaly, or
atelectasis can provide a different explanation from the COVID-19 pattern."

Say for Normal:
"The normal case should show low evidence for most pathology concepts and a
stronger clear-lung signal. This lets us inspect whether the system's
explanation changes coherently with the input rather than repeating a generic
text template."

Significance:
"This is the first interpretability benefit: two images can receive related
high-level labels but for different concept reasons, and the user can inspect
those reasons directly."

Presenter note:
Do not claim that a concept score is a radiologist finding. Say 'the model's
concept score' or 'model-derived evidence'.

2. Show model and explanation reliability (1 minute)
-----------------------------------------------------
Action:
1. Expand Model Trust.
2. Point to Task Head: held-out test accuracy, macro-AUC, and calibrated ECE.
3. Point to per-class AUC.
4. Point to Concept Probe Reliability.
5. Point to Concept Surrogate Fidelity.

Say:
"The trust panel separates three different questions. First, how well does
the task head classify held-out images? Second, how well does each concept
probe identify its concept? Third, how faithfully can the concept vector
reproduce the black-box prediction?"

Point out the current medical values:
- Task head test accuracy: about 81%.
- Macro-AUC: about 0.94.
- Calibrated ECE: about 0.02.
- Concept probe test AUC: approximately 0.87-0.98 after the expanded bank.
- Concept surrogate test fidelity: about 79%.

Say:
"Fidelity is not classification accuracy. It is agreement between the
concept-only surrogate and the original black-box head on held-out images.
A fidelity of about 79% means the concept explanation captures a substantial
part of the model behavior, but not all of it. That limitation is visible
instead of being hidden."

Significance:
"This prevents explanation overclaiming. A good-looking heatmap is not enough;
we also show whether the concept layer is predictive and whether it faithfully
tracks the underlying model."

3. Show concept-specific spatial evidence (2 minutes)
-------------------------------------------------------
Action:
1. Return to the COVID-19 sample and click Predict if needed.
2. Click the Ground Glass Opacity concept's attribution button.
3. Wait for the heatmap and overlay.
4. Compare the original image, heatmap, and overlay.
5. Click a second concept such as Cardiomegaly or Pleural Effusion.

Say:
"This is not one generic saliency map. The attribution target is the selected
concept. For Ground Glass Opacity, the probe coefficients weight the DenseNet
feature maps directly, producing probe-grounded spatial evidence."

Then say:
"When I change the target concept, the evidence map changes. That is the key
 distinction from showing one generic heatmap and attaching different labels
afterwards."

Significance:
"The interface connects a human concept to image regions: what concept was
used, where the model found supporting evidence, and how strong the probe is.
This makes the explanation inspectable rather than purely narrative."

Careful wording:
Use 'the model attends to regions consistent with this concept'. Do not say
that the heatmap proves a lesion is present or that it localizes disease with
clinical accuracy. There is no radiologist localization ground truth in this
demo.

4. Demonstrate a controlled counterfactual (2-3 minutes)
----------------------------------------------------------
Action:
1. Keep the COVID-19 sample selected.
2. Run Predict and click Reset Concepts if the sliders were changed.
3. In the concept panel, lower Ground Glass Opacity and Bilateral Involvement.
4. Click Apply Changes.
5. Optionally restore the concepts and instead lower Pleural Effusion or
   Cardiomegaly to show that different interventions have different effects.

Say before changing sliders:
"This is an intervention on the explanation layer, not an edited medical
image. I am asking: if the concept evidence changed, how would the learned
surrogate's prediction change?"

Say after applying:
"The prediction and confidence change because the concept vector has changed.
The delta panel shows exactly which concepts were modified. This gives us a
counterfactual explanation: which evidence would need to be weaker or
stronger for the model's decision to change?"

Significance:
"Counterfactuals turn explanation into an interactive hypothesis test. They
are useful for debugging and understanding model behavior, but they do not
mean we can safely remove or add pathology in a real patient image."

If the label does not flip:
"The model did not cross its decision boundary under this intervention. That
is still informative: the selected concepts were not sufficient to change
the decision at this confidence level."

Closing (30 seconds)
---------------------
Say:
"The demo has shown four layers: the prediction, the concept fingerprint,
concept-specific spatial attribution, and an explicit counterfactual. The
main significance is that the system exposes both evidence and limitations:
we can quantify task performance, concept reliability, and surrogate fidelity
instead of presenting an explanation with no reliability signal."

End with:
"The next validation step is clinical annotation and external evaluation. The
current prototype uses weak/model-derived concept labels and should support
research inspection, not clinical decision-making."

Fallback plan if a model is slow
---------------------------------
- Use the preloaded COVID-19 sample first.
- Keep the Model Trust panel collapsed until after the first prediction.
- Do not switch models during the presentation.
- If a heatmap request is slow, narrate the target concept and wait; do not
  infer a result from the class prediction alone.
- If the live model fails, show the Trust panel and metrics report, then
  explain that the demo separates offline evaluation from live inference.

One-slide takeaway
-------------------
Prediction: What class did the model choose?
Concepts: What human-interpretable evidence supports it?
Attribution: Where in the image did that concept evidence arise?
Counterfactual: What evidence would need to change the decision?
Trust: How reliable and faithful is the explanation?
